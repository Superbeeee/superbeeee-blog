As a hardcore On fan, I'd been wanting to try this shoe ever since it launched last summer. Stock has always been scarce: the first colorway was the Paris Olympics edition (I'm not even sure it was sold publicly — QQ); the second was the lime colorway, released last April, only available at On Labs locations, flagship stores, and select retailers; the third dropped on 9/4 this year alongside the opening of the Tokyo Ginza flagship — white-and-raspberry. There's a small easter egg about the colors at the end of this post, so stick around. ^0^

From what I've observed, On doesn't really push online sales for the LS. The main reason is probably that this shoe really needs to be tried on and test-run, and at this price point physical retail handles demand better. That said, I've noticed the US site does occasionally stock small sizes. Bottom line: this is a shoe you have to be lucky to land. (tears)

## 1. Introduction

The first impression most people have of the LS is the prototype Hellen Obiri wore at the 2024 Boston Marathon, where she took the women's title.

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/12/on-cloudboom-strike-ls-review/01-obiri-prototype.jpg" alt="Hellen Obiri at the Boston Marathon in On Cloudboom Strike LS Dev1.2">
<figcaption>Hellen Obiri at the Boston Marathon in On Cloudboom Strike LS Dev1.2</figcaption>
</figure>

LS stands for LightSpray™ — On Labs' patented robotic-arm precision spray-coating process. A robotic arm literally sprays the upper directly onto the shoe.

The origin story is fun. The inventor, Johannes Voelchert, said that as a student he saw a hot-glue Halloween decoration that sprayed something resembling a spider web — and was struck by the idea that you could spray textiles into complex shapes the same way. He brought the concept to the Salone del Mobile in Milan, On caught wind of it, the leadership team met with him, and they invited him to join.

On says it took five years of R&D. The upper is sprayed from 1.5 km of thermoplastic fiber, dyed and branded in three minutes, then heat-bonded to the sole. They also claim the process and materials drastically reduce the carbon footprint of shoe manufacturing — sustainability angle included.

Getting hold of a pair came down to a friend traveling in Japan spotting stock at Xeibo and finding — incredibly — my golden size, US10!!

Official LS pricing: USD 330 (pre-tax), NTD 11,800 (tax-included), JPY 44,000 (tax-included).

Big thanks to my friend who used Xeibo's tax-refund (On's flagship stores don't offer one — QQ) plus a 5% promo, bringing it down to JPY 38,589 — about NTD 7,788 at the current rate. They even hauled the box back for me. (kneels)

### Shoebox

### Side profile

The LS's biggest break with convention is the absence of laces and a tongue — it's one piece. That'd be acceptable for a casual shoe, but the fact that you can race a marathon in something built like this is a real breakthrough. The midsole (39.5mm/35.5mm, 4mm drop) stacks two layers of On's top-tier Helion HF (Hyper Foam), a Pebax-based foam made with over 40% bio-based material, with a Speedboard (On's carbon plate) sandwiched between for propulsion.

<img src="/post/2025/12/on-cloudboom-strike-ls-review/07-midsole.jpg" alt="Midsole structure">

As for the iconic CloudTec® holes — given this is a race shoe, they've become more symbolic than functional. Open them up too much and propulsion stability suffers. Fun trivia: the original prototype, LS Dev1, had three holes; the production version has four.

The outsole carries On's seam-line mini-logo, with different tread patterns on the inner vs. outer heel — a nice touch. The midfoot is stamped with international sizing references, and on the forefoot the diagonal grooves are denser on the outer edge and thin out toward the inside, giving the high-wear zones more rubber to chew through.

## 3. On-Foot Feel & Sizing

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/12/on-cloudboom-strike-ls-review/13-on-foot.jpg" alt="On-foot shot">
<figcaption>📸 @kevinchung0829</figcaption>

### First impressions

Once you've practiced the technique, getting them on is fast. The standing feel is reminiscent of the Ray — light and comfortable. With no laces, the instep wraps in a more form-fitting way; the heel collar has no padding but offers solid structural support, which actually sidesteps the Achilles-rubbing issue some Strikes have.

### Sizing & fit

Sizing was a long deliberation. There's no place in Taiwan to try them on, and I didn't run into a try-on opportunity on my recent trip abroad. On officially says LS sizing is consistent with the laced Strike, but without laces to fine-tune, the choice matters way more. Buying blind is risky. I'd also seen Zhe-Rui mention at the Tokyo Marathon Expo that he thought a half-size up was right — which only added to the doubt.

I went with my usual gamble and stuck with US 10 (28cm)!

Lucky again — the fit is on. My navicular bone protrudes a bit more than average, so I was worried about rubbing on long runs. Also, the toebox isn't as wide as I'd imagined; even at the same nominal size, the laced Strike feels wider in the forefoot. Both points only really got tested on the run.

<img src="/post/2025/12/on-cloudboom-strike-ls-review/14-fitting.jpg" alt="Fit check">

### Weight

A year ago, the LS's weight would have been jaw-dropping. But this year is a free-for-all in racing shoes: ASICS' Ray and Tokyo Sky, Puma's R-3, and recently the Mizuno HYPERWARP series have all generated buzz. So purely on weight, the LS is light, but it doesn't lead the pack like it would have last year.

## 4. Run Testing

### a. Track intervals

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/12/on-cloudboom-strike-ls-review/16-track-interval.jpg" alt="Track intervals at Beitian">
<figcaption>📸 @kevinchung0829</figcaption>
</figure>

**Location:** Beitian
**Distance:** 4km × 2
**Pace:** 4:10/km (4km × 2, r 180s, 1:40/400m ± 2)

When the LS first launched I assumed it was just a laceless Strike with reduced weight. The run proved otherwise. Starting with longer intervals — I'm a long-stride runner — the moment I took off I noticed the feedback was very different from the laced Strike. The Strike rewards a long stride with a clear bouncy return; the LS reminded me more of the Nike Vaporfly Next% 2 — firmer, with steadier feedback. It clicked that the LS is probably better suited to higher-cadence runners. That said, since the PU track has its own bounce, I could still hold pace under acceleration — which made me even more curious about how it'd feel on the road.

### b. Road flat (long run | tempo)

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/12/on-cloudboom-strike-ls-review/17-tempo-run.jpg" alt="Tempo at Dadaocheng riverside">
<figcaption>📸 @kevinchung0829</figcaption>
</figure>

**Location:** Dazhi riverside | Dadaocheng riverside
**Distance:** 21K | 12K
**Pace:** 4:30-4:35/km | 4:25/km

I split road testing between tempo and long. During the Dadaocheng tempo, the LS still didn't bounce back like the Strike — it's a steady-output shoe. There was a side note: Dadaocheng has been under construction, and a water truck was spraying down dust mid-run. To keep the new shoes from getting drenched I tiptoed across each puddle. Bouncing on the forefoot to clear the water, I found the LS's sweet spot — slightly further forward than I'd expected. Once I'd located it and adjusted my form, the propulsion really kicked in. At 4:25 pace I unintentionally drifted down to 4:10. The shoe is genuinely tuned for forefoot strikers.

For the Dazhi long run I deliberately tweaked stride and cadence to stay on the sweet spot. The transitions worked, but the form wasn't natural to me, and a few days later I had unusual soreness in the right hip and the medial side of the left foot. The LS can train your cadence up and pull your foot strike forward, but those aren't habits you flip overnight.

## 5. Honest Pros & Cons

### Pros

Beyond the steady propulsion and the light weight, the standout for me is the no-lace design. Once you've got the on-foot technique down, the LS slips on instantly — at the 2025 IRONMAN World Championship, both Gustav Iden and Kristian Blummenfelt got them on smoothly in transition. No laces means no laces-too-tight, no laces-too-loose, and no laces-coming-undone-mid-race. The upper, navicular issues aside, hugs without hardness — minimal rubbing risk. Pick the right size and it's a reliable marathon shoe for steady output.

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/12/on-cloudboom-strike-ls-review/19-pros.jpg" alt="LS pros">
<figcaption>📸 @kevinchung0829</figcaption>
</figure>

### Cons

When people think of LS downsides, durability is usually the first guess. I haven't crossed 100 km yet, but several international runners have shared positive durability impressions — the upper holds up and the outsole wears as well as the laced Strike's. The real downsides: the LS is genuinely hard to buy, and you really, really should try it on first. Without laces to compensate, sizing has to be right. The design also doesn't reward long-stride runners as much. And the price is steep (without the JPY exchange-rate cushion, anyway, XD). If you just want to experience an On carbon shoe, the laced Strike is more than enough.

## 6. Who Is This Shoe For?

After intervals and long runs, my conclusion is: forefoot strikers with a higher cadence. The propulsion sweet spot sits forward, and when I overstrode I got less return than from the Strike — the harder I tried, the worse my efficiency got. Switching to higher cadence flipped the experience entirely.

**Summary:** Runners who want a quick on-foot, no laces, with higher cadence and forefoot striking.

## 7. Would I Recommend It?

The LS is positioned for high-speed runners; combined with limited supply and a steep price, it's a high-barrier shoe. But experiencing the LightSpray tech firsthand is genuinely novel — if you can land a pair, I do recommend trying this take on shoe construction.

Even though the LS has been out for over a year, On keeps testing the tech in different shoes — like the recently-talked-about LS variant trickling down to the Cloudmonster 3 Hyper. There's a reasonable chance this tech makes its way into more shoes and shifts the broader industry.
