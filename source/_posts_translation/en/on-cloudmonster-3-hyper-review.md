## 1. Introduction

Mention On and the first thing most people think of is the looks — the styling, the streetwear appeal. But over the past few years On's ambition in serious performance running has been impossible to miss. As the market's "super trainer" segment turns into an all-out war, On has formally sent in its new contender — the **Cloudmonster 3 Hyper**. As the step-up member of the Monster family (leaving the LS big brother out of this for now, ha), the Cloudmonster 3 Hyper is positioned as a max-stack, plateless all-round trainer that sits between daily easy miles and carbon-plated racers.

Design-wise, it boldly removes On's own Speedboard and swaps in a 40 mm-plus dual-layer midsole. The top layer is generously loaded with the same **Helion HF (PEBA) supercritical foam** used in On's top-tier racers, chasing maximum softness and energy return; the bottom layer keeps the firmer everyday-training EVA foam along with the hollow CloudTec pods to anchor the platform underfoot.

That said, at NT$7,280 (US$220 | ¥27,500), recreational runners are going to ask the obvious question: is this shoe all show, or is it a genuine weapon for making long runs and tempo work more efficient? Let's find out what's actually in the bottle.

The upper uses zoned knitting, but even at its thinnest you can't see through to your sock or the interior. The tongue is connected by a fabric layer, so once the laces are cinched you don't get that strangled feeling across the top of the foot. But because this shoe lives between daily trainer and carbon racer, you can feel that it's neither as plush as a standard easy-day upper nor as airy as a race-day one.

### Outsole

<figure>
<img src="/post/2026/07/on-cloudmonster-3-hyper-review/08-outsole.jpg" alt="On Cloudmonster 3 Hyper outsole and CloudTec pods">
<figcaption>📸 @kevinchung0829</figcaption>
</figure>

The Hyper 3 uses more premium foam than the original Hyper — the same PEBAX material found in the Strike — which shaves off some weight. Underneath, though, the CloudTec pods are still there (On's insistence, ha), and they add a bit of resistance when you're accelerating: the pods create an extra layer of compression space, so each toe-off takes longer to rebound than it would in a racer.

Most of On's racing shoes run Helion foam under the full foot and push the CloudTec pods toward the heel. That symbolic design has always forced a trade-off in On's carbon-plated shoes, because cutting holes in the sole inevitably costs you stability under acceleration.

> "So On is forever tuning the ratio between foam and CloudTec."

Putting CloudTec under the Hyper sacrifices acceleration, but it noticeably raises the shoe's stability — especially on the medial side, where it reins in arch collapse and gives you real support. Before your foot can pronate into the acceleration, the medial pods prop it up first; that's a third-gen-only tuning. The flip side is that the Hyper 3's sole is on the firmer side, so it may take a few runs to break in.

## 3. On-Foot Feel & Sizing

### First Impressions

<figure>
<img src="/post/2026/07/on-cloudmonster-3-hyper-review/10-on-foot.jpg" alt="On Cloudmonster 3 Hyper on foot">
<figcaption>📸 @kevinchung0829</figcaption>
</figure>

Having tried the standard Monster 3, putting on the Hyper 3 makes the weight difference immediately obvious — it's much lighter, and the heel lockdown is substantial enough to feel genuinely secure.

The drop also felt noticeably steep. I looked it up: On lists this shoe at 6 mm, but RunRepeat's cut-in-half lab test found the true drop is actually 9.2 mm — which matches my on-foot impression exactly. It gives you a much stronger sense of rolling forward.

That said, after wearing them a while, the upper — comfortable and nicely finished as it is — does get a little warm. This might be a shoe better suited to fall and winter training.

## 4. Run Testing

### a. Flat Road (Tempo Run)

**Location:** Dadaocheng riverside
**Distance:** 10K
**Pace:** 5:00/km

For the outdoor test I went with a tempo run. Once up to target pace, the stability this shoe delivers is unmistakable — as mentioned above, the medial CloudTec reinforcement means you don't have to worry about pronating as fatigue sets in, and every stride links smoothly into the next. Even in the summer heat, holding the rhythm was straightforward. The one catch is that the CloudTec setup makes the sole a bit rigid, so it feels slightly clunky through turns and detours. Setting that aside, training on straight stretches was genuinely excellent.

### b. Track Intervals

**Location:** Dalong Elementary School
**Distance:** 600m × 4
**Pace:** 4:00/km (600m × 4, 180s recovery, 1:36/400m ± 2)

I already knew this shoe's positioning meant it probably couldn't handle paces this fast, but I wanted to run some intervals to feel it out anyway. Once up to speed you can definitely feel each toe-off lacking the big propulsive kick of a carbon racer — it's as if every step gets caught by the space inside the CloudTec pods, so the return isn't as pronounced. I tried shifting my footstrike further forward to avoid the concentrated pod zone, and there still wasn't any notable propulsion (the plate is gone, after all) — confirming that this shoe is probably happiest between 5:00 and 4:50 per km.

## 5. The Brutal Pros & Cons

### Pros ✅

The Hyper 3's strength is that after dropping the signature Speedboard, it's fully loaded with the Helion HF (PEBA) supercritical foam from On's top racers, which solves the firm-underfoot problem of the past. RunRepeat's lab data puts heel and forefoot energy return at 66.1% and 69.4% respectively — a genuinely lively rebound. Some runners have even tested lower oxygen consumption and lower blood lactate at the same pace while wearing the Hyper 3.

Beyond that, the max-stack cushioning is real: RunRepeat's cut test measured actual heel stack at 43.4 mm and forefoot at 34.2 mm, giving serious impact protection for long-distance training — other reviewers have described it as "the grand tourer of running shoes." Add the flared design outside the first metatarsal, which effectively props up the foot and prevents overpronation, and its dynamic performance ranks near the top of the super-trainer field.

### Cons ❌

Every time On's downsides come up, price is inevitably on the list (expensive has never been its problem — it's mine...). Positioned as a super trainer, it's up against the ASICS Super Blast 3 and Brooks Hyperion Max 3, both excellent shoes.

But I can offer a partial defense of the price this time. I haven't put a hundred miles on them yet, but RunRepeat's brutal Dremel abrasion test wore only 0.5 mm off the outsole rubber (the average running shoe loses 1.1 mm), and the ankle-collar foam also scored high on durability — well ahead of most competitors. Maybe durability is where it wins a point back this round ^0^.

## 6. What Kind of Runner Is This Shoe For?

This shoe suits **tempo work and long endurance training**. For runners around a 3:30 marathon, it's a great fit for tempo runs at 4:50–5:00/km. Because of the CloudTec pods, though, go any faster — inside 4:40 — and the ride starts feeling a little unsettled.

The bump in stack also meaningfully improves cushioning, which makes it a good match for heavier runners looking to reduce load on the knees; and the plateless build is a solid choice for everyday training too.

## 7. Would I Recommend It?

On looks alone, once again... yes, recommended!

One more fun test from overseas: the Hyper 3 laughs at cold weather. Thanks to the PEBA top layer, it stays remarkably consistent in extreme conditions. After 20 minutes in a lab freezer, its midsole foam stiffened by only 7% (the average running shoe stiffens by 22%), meaning even in deep winter it keeps that excellent soft-and-bouncy feel (reading this at 30°C in Taiwan is refreshing in itself, ha).
