## 🌟 Quick Intro

This year's target race had always been Taipei Marathon. I ended up running Fukuoka because a friend recommended it back in May — said Fukuoka was a fun trip — so I poked around for any running events, found Fukuoka Marathon registration was open, and figured why not. To my surprise, I got picked in the lottery. Off we go.

## 🌟 EXPO

<img src="/post/2024/12/fukuoka-marathon-2024-debut/02-expo-venue.jpg" alt="EXPO venue">

The EXPO was at the plaza in front of city hall. Picking up the bib I bumped into a Taiwanese staff member who walked me through the meeting and bag-drop logistics. After bib pickup, the rear tent area had a few brand booths. Generous swag — but only runners could enter, so my friends had to wait outside (sorry XD). I remember spotting McDonald's, Maurten, On, ASICS.

First-marathon nerves: woke up around 4am and kept startling awake afraid I'd miss the start XD.

Got up, packed in order, headed out!

<img src="/post/2024/12/fukuoka-marathon-2024-debut/11-pre-race.jpg" alt="Pre-race prep">

At the venue I warmed up, asked a passerby for a photo, dropped my bag, and went to find the toilets — only to find massive queues. As my turn approached, a staff member shouted that I could jump straight in (because if the line moved you'd have to start over from the back), and I made it into corral E just in time. (I later learned this is standard practice at Japanese road races.)

## 🌟 The Race

It took about 5 minutes to cross the start line — partly because of crowding. The first kilometers were conservative; you can't really push through the pack. Whether from excitement or my first try with caffeine pills, I needed a pit stop around 5K. From 6–7K things felt steady. Temperatures were great — historical averages run 8–12°C; we got 15–20°C, but with light rain it was fine. Once stable, I settled in around 5:00/km and the data later showed the first 32K went smoothly. After that the hills and a stretch of coast turned suddenly muggy and pace slipped a bit.

I'd assumed support would thin out once we left the city, but even in the back half through Kyushu University and the suburban streets, plenty of locals were out cheering. Elderly folks standing in the cold, people in wheelchairs — I gave thumbs-ups to each one, grateful for the encouragement. I also kept reading the signs people held — sometimes "go for it," sometimes a family member's name — a useful distraction from fatigue. XD

Around 38K it hit hard. I wanted to pick up the pace but couldn't. The body's battery was just out. Quads close to cramping. I downed a Maurten white packet and walked through the last aid station, which helped a lot. On the final straight to the finish, my headphones died. Genuine panic — music is a real psychological prop for me — but I held form and crossed the line.

To me:

> Marathon —
> not about cardio, not about lungs;
> about spirit, about willpower.

<img src="/post/2024/12/fukuoka-marathon-2024-debut/12-finish-time.jpg" alt="Finish time">

At the line I felt small inside this sport. For the first time I dropped to my knees — knew this is something massive, and so much support from countless people had helped me get there. Genuinely grateful.

<img src="/post/2024/12/fukuoka-marathon-2024-debut/13-finisher-cert.jpg" alt="Finisher certificate">

**Chip time: 3:38:11**

The pre-race target was 3:39 and I never imagined hitting 3:38-something. There are too many people to thank — I'll get to them all another time.

## 🌟 Fukuoka Marathon Tips

### 1. Cargopass

Once you arrive at Hakata Station from the airport, you can use [Cargopass](https://cargopass.jp/hotel) to ship your luggage to your hotel — straight to EXPO afterwards.

I didn't end up using it because my first-night hotel was right next to the venue. If your travel partners aren't, this is a great service.

### 2. Shuttle Tickets & Official Hotels

The "shuttle ticket" refers to the paid post-finish shuttle from the finish back to Hakata Station.

The free shuttle only takes you to the nearest subway from the finish.

For full-marathon runners who just want to sit and rest after, the paid shuttle is a lifesaver. (Tears.)

<img src="/post/2024/12/fukuoka-marathon-2024-debut/16-shuttle-info.jpg" alt="Shuttle/official-hotel info">

I'd read fellow runners' Fukuoka writeups beforehand. In years past you had to pre-print the ticket at a Japanese convenience store — inconvenient if you don't have friends in Japan. In 2023 they sold them at EXPO. This year the official line was that shuttle tickets were bundled with the official partner hotel package — meaning you could only get them by booking those hotels.

After finishing, on my way to the paid shuttle line, I noticed they were actually selling tickets on-site too — but I can't promise next year's policy will be the same. QQ

(Side note: at the hotel front desk I had a long back-and-forth trying to confirm my package included shuttle tickets. The clerk wasn't familiar with the program; only after escalating to the manager did they find a stack of red envelopes — those were the runner shuttle tickets.)

## 🌟 Reflections

My target was always Taipei Marathon. When I entered the Fukuoka lottery I didn't realize the dates were so close. My coach pointed out I really shouldn't push hard, since Taipei was only a month later. But this was both my first marathon and my first overseas race, and I wanted to do it right — every detail. To run this performance is, for me, more than enough. Fukuoka's weather and course are very runner-friendly, the logistics tight, marshals stationed every section. The crowd support was way warmer than Taipei — felt like the whole city was in on it. I love Fukuoka Marathon's tagline:

**"Connect people, connect cities, connect to the future."**

**「人をつなぐ。まちをつなぐ。未来へつなぐ。」**

Beyond friends and family, students and elderly folks all came out to cheer — really moving. In an aging country like Japan, maybe this is one of the ways the social fabric stays connected. I hope Taipei Marathon can move toward this someday too. (Probably starts too early — half the city's still asleep.)

If you're chasing a time, going through a tour agency probably saves a lot of headache. But Fukuoka only opens 500 lottery spots for overseas runners and (afaik) tour agencies don't get those slots — so if you really want to run it, hopefully this post helps. XD
