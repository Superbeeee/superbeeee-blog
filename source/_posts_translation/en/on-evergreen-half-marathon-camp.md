(My very first piece on Sports Note goes to the On running camp, of course.)

As a hardcore On fan, the moment I heard about a brand-run training camp I rushed to apply. Lucky enough to clear the written round and land an in-person interview, where I added more brand-history and material research (shoutout to my fellow ONON Holding shareholders — I even tracked down the Taiwan-based manufacturer of the relevant material XDD). And then — got picked.

## Gear Pack & Cost

The biggest hook of the camp is the gear pack you get when accepted: On Performance apparel, an On lightweight running cap, and the On Cloudsurfer Next — total retail value $12,020 — plus ten coaching sessions. Registration was $3,600. Genuinely a steal (Hakka frugal mode: ON).

## Training Program

<img src="/post/2024/11/on-evergreen-half-marathon-camp/03-schedule.png" alt="Ten-week training camp schedule">

Across the ten weeks, the toughest sessions for me were:

- **Week 1: the heat-bake 5K time trial**

<img src="/post/2024/11/on-evergreen-half-marathon-camp/04-week1-5km.jpg" alt="">

- **Week 5: the An-sen orienteering session in pouring rain**

<img src="/post/2024/11/on-evergreen-half-marathon-camp/05-week5-orienteering.png" alt="">

- **Week 6: the Jian-zhong-jian boss hill**

<img src="/post/2024/11/on-evergreen-half-marathon-camp/06-week6-hill.png" alt="">

Plus ten consecutive Saturdays starting at 5–6am. But Coach Wei-Hsiang made the sessions fun, and the assistant coaches were attentive throughout — **emotional value** delivered in full, so I never felt the work was draining.

<img src="/post/2024/11/on-evergreen-half-marathon-camp/07-group-photo.jpg" alt="">

The other key piece is the personalized training plan. After the Week 1 5K trial, the coach split everyone into five groups — I was placed in Group 3. Weekly plans varied with the training focus. Since I had other races coming up, I layered in my own additional plans on top.

<img src="/post/2024/11/on-evergreen-half-marathon-camp/08-weekly-training.png" alt="Sample weekly training plan">

Before race day, Coach Wei-Hsiang projected finish times for each runner based on form. Mine landed in the **1:51–1:56** range. My previous PB was 1:52, set in winter under good rest and great conditions. With humid summer weather looming, I had real doubts.

## Race Day

Race morning didn't feel great. Reminiscent of this year's Nat Geo Run — muggy, high humidity.

<img src="/post/2024/11/on-evergreen-half-marathon-camp/09-race-day.jpg" alt="Race day morning">

After warming up with the camp, I lined up. Right as I crossed the starting mat — my watch crashed. Instant panic. "How am I going to track HR in this heat?" "How do I check pace?" I considered just running easy and enjoying the scenery, but after weeks of dedicated training I really wanted the data. Mercifully, after a kilometer of random button-mashing the watch came back. Reset, refocus, race.

HR ran high all day — partially on me for skimping on sleep. In the final 2K an NRC pacer pulled alongside, kept encouraging me, helped me reset breathing and form, and I held on with desperate smiles at every photographer (essential survival skill).

<img src="/post/2024/11/on-evergreen-half-marathon-camp/10-finish-sprint.jpg" alt="Finish sprint">

## Result

Final time: **1:48:03** — **"Beat my own. And that was my winter self."** Beat my own and beat the coach's projection. **Comparing yourself to yourself is honestly the most fun thing.**

<img src="/post/2024/11/on-evergreen-half-marathon-camp/11-result.png" alt="Race result">

What a summer — packed and shaped entirely by running. Looking at this month's calendar, it's a bit unhinged. But great fun.

## Bonus — Brand Collaboration Easter Egg

The race kit, by the way? Loewe × On. The two brands have been collaborating for a while — it's already their sixth drop. The fifth (earlier this year) had a jaw-dropper: the two logos actually merged into one.

<img src="/post/2024/11/on-evergreen-half-marathon-camp/13-loewe-on-outfit.jpg" alt="Loewe × On collaboration kit">

I called every counter at the time, only to learn each country had just one boutique displaying the items. In Taiwan it was the Loewe Breeze Nanshan store. After buying I had no idea where I'd actually wear it — until I realized it was perfectly suited to the camp's target race.

<img src="/post/2024/11/on-evergreen-half-marathon-camp/14-loewe-on-logo.png" alt="Loewe × On merged logo">

Going further, the deeper origin probably traces to *Challengers* — the Luca Guadagnino film. Beyond being a tennis movie (relevant given On's tennis line, with Federer joining the brand's research team in 2019), the film's costumes were designed by Jonathan Anderson, then Loewe's creative director.

What's even more fun: at this year's Olympics, On set up a LightSpray™ technology installation in Paris showcasing the Cloudboom Strike LS (the same prototype Hellen Obiri wore at Boston, where she won). *Challengers* lead Zendaya showed up to try it on — at the time I assumed she was just visiting, but soon after, On announced her as a brand ambassador.

<img src="/post/2024/11/on-evergreen-half-marathon-camp/15-cloudboom-strike-ls.jpg" alt="On Cloudboom Strike LS">

<img src="/post/2024/11/on-evergreen-half-marathon-camp/16-zendaya.jpg" alt="Zendaya trying on the shoe">

Recently On opened LightSpray™ technology showrooms in Shanghai and New York where you can try the shoe on (envious tears).

More fun races coming in the second half of the year — onward. If you've got On info to share, hit me up.
