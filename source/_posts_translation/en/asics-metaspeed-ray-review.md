ASICS officially entered the carbon-plate racing arena with the **METASPEED** line in 2021. From Sky/Edge (2021) ➡️ + series (2023) ➡️ Paris series (2024) ➡️ Tokyo series (2025), and now the star of this post — the "Ray."

The official copy: **The newest member of the METASPEED line, METASPEED RAY, takes its name from the speed of light — fused with the Japanese "Rei (零)" for "zero" — symbolizing extreme lightness and breakthrough speed.**

## 2. Unboxing & Design ✨

The shoes arrived a day later in a giant ASICS carton, with a complimentary shoe bag included for hitting the order threshold.

**The first thing that grabs you is the absurdly tall stack.** The gradient sidewall has "FFLeap" printed on it — that's the new FF LEAP elastic compound used in the midsole. Paired with a matching FF LEAP insole, the forefoot's push gets cleanly returned, balancing punch with comfort.

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/08/asics-metaspeed-ray-review/07-side-view.jpg" alt="Inside/outside view of the high stack">
<figcaption>📸 @kevinchung0829</figcaption>
</figure>

The carbon plate runs only the front three-quarters of the shoe, paired with the FF LEAP™ foam. ASICS C-PROJECT's lead Takemura had this to say:

> "The Stride and the Edge aren't necessarily designed for forefoot/midfoot and heel strikers, respectively. You could be a heel striker, for example, but with longer, loping strides, and therefore lean more towards the Sky. Therefore, the Ray, 'could be for both'."

The point being: unlike Sky/Edge, the Ray isn't pinned to a specific footstrike. Long-stride or high-cadence — both can extract value from it.

**The signature tiger stripes on the sidewall** signal speed (Onitsuka Tiger!), with this season's "Tokyo" branding stamped just behind — extremely high series identity. Laces are the now-standard textured anti-slip type.

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/08/asics-metaspeed-ray-review/08-tiger-stripe.jpg" alt="Sidewall tiger stripes with Tokyo branding">
<figcaption>📸 @kevinchung0829</figcaption>

I'd already tried the left shoe on at home and taken a few steps; you can already see fine wear marks on the outsole. Once they hit the road for real, the marks will be obvious.

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/08/asics-metaspeed-ray-review/09-outsole-marks.jpg" alt="Outsole wear marks after a few indoor steps">

**The upper uses the latest MATRYX® yarn technology** — visibly thin enough that it feels like one careless move could rip it. Once on, you can easily see what color socks you're wearing through it. The toe tip isn't reinforced, so I'm a bit worried about long toenails punching through.

<img src="/post/2025/08/asics-metaspeed-ray-review/10-upper-matryx.jpg" alt="MATRYX® yarn upper">

**The outsole is dominated by white rubber — covering nearly two-thirds of the area.** First reaction: is the grip going to be enough? The heel especially has only a small grip patch on the inside. The patch itself feels grippy in hand, but it does bring to mind the incident at this year's World Masters Games where a Japanese athlete in EVO 1 slipped on a chip mat.

## 3. On-Foot Feel & Sizing

**First impressions**

The first words out of my mouth were "incredible." The lightness is immediate, and the upper is genuinely comfortable. Because of the slope built into the design, just standing in them feels slightly off-balance, and walking is almost like trudging through mud — the metaphor is exaggerated, but it captures the Ray's identity: extreme lightness plus aggressive propulsion.

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/08/asics-metaspeed-ray-review/15-on-foot.jpg" alt="On-foot first impression">
<figcaption>📸 @kevinchung0829</figcaption>
</figure>

**Sizing & fit**

For most brands I take US 10 or 28 cm. ASICS-friendly friends warned me that ASICS shoes usually need a half-size up — but I bought online and didn't have time for a try-on, so I went with my standard US 10.

I tried them on with bated breath when they arrived. Thankfully, US 10 turned out right. My feet aren't unusually wide; the Ray's toebox shape reminded me of the fourth-gen Nike Vaporfly. Same US 10, plenty of room in front of the toes.

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/08/asics-metaspeed-ray-review/16-side-profile.JPG" alt="Ray side profile">
<figcaption>📸 @kevinchung0829</figcaption>

**Weight**

Straight to the ultralight carbon-plate leaderboard — light, no other word for it. Official weight is 129g for US 9, with overseas runners reporting around 136g for US 10.

## 4. Run Testing

### a. Road flat

**Location:** Dadaocheng riverside
**Distance:** 15K
**Pace:** 4:40/km

<img src="/post/2025/08/asics-metaspeed-ray-review/18-dadaocheng-long.jpg" alt="15K long run at Dadaocheng riverside">

I tested the long-run feel along Dadaocheng riverside. Before starting, I noticed something — possibly because the carbon plate is only 3/4 length, my toes felt a bit "suspended." That sensation faded once I started running.

The propulsion is intense, almost daring you to surge. Through the first 5 km I could clearly feel the bounce and the push; past 5 km I'd settled into the rhythm — not that the bounce had faded, more that shoe and foot had merged.

The whole long run felt fluid: light shoe, comfortable upper, smooth step-to-step transitions. The earlier worry did show up, though — cornering. At higher speeds, the soft midsole is a bit unstable through curves.

### b. Track intervals

**Location:** Beitian
**Distance:** 1km × 4
**Pace:** 4:00/km (1km × 4, r 180s, 1:36/400m ± 2)

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/08/asics-metaspeed-ray-review/19-track-interval.jpg" alt="1km × 4 intervals at Beitian">
<figcaption>📸 @kevinchung0829</figcaption>

Coming off the long run I'd already adapted to the Ray's feedback. At higher speed the propulsion is full-on, and the track's flat lanes plus tolerable curves let the Ray sidestep its weakness. If I had to nitpick: the track already has its own bounce, and the FF LEAP feels almost too springy on top of it. It reminded me of trying the Adidas ADIOS PRO 4 — bouncy foam plus bouncy track is a bit much. On the road it'd feel more harmonious.

## 5. Honest Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

The FF LEAP foam plus the carbon plate's deliberate placement give speed-oriented runners stable feedback. The era-defining lightness leaves a strong impression — go back to a normal pair afterward and it feels like you can't go back. Even amateur runners with reasonable mileage can adapt to the Ray's feel quickly.

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/08/asics-metaspeed-ray-review/21-pros.jpg" alt="Ray pros — FF LEAP feedback and lightness">
<figcaption>📸 @kevinchung0829</figcaption>
</figure>

**Cons:**

The big one is cornering stability — you either need the technique or you slow down. That tilts this shoe toward straight-line-heavy races. Then there's the price. The Ray is the natural EVO 1 competitor, and while it's cheaper than the EVO 1, it's still meaningfully above other carbon racers.

Extreme lightness and durability rarely correlate. Takemura did say the Ray is built to deliver more than one marathon's worth of quality kilometers, but the featherweight foam and gauzy upper still raise legitimate durability questions.

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/08/asics-metaspeed-ray-review/22-cons.jpg" alt="Ray cons — cornering, price, durability">

## 6. Who Is This Shoe For?

Takemura said the Ray isn't restricted to high-cadence or long-stride runners, but the forward-shifted plate clearly favors forefoot strikers. And as I mentioned, the FF LEAP feedback is so insistent that even standing or strolling tilts you forward — confirming that the Ray is built for speed-focused athletes.

**Summary: "For runners chasing extreme lightness, who land on the forefoot and run at speed."**

## 7. Would I Recommend It?

Yes — but your wallet has to be willing.

Since ASICS spun up its C-PROJECT team to run "Project TOP," their ambition and output have been increasingly visible. The METASPEED edge/sky line keeps generating chatter, the Tokyo "facepaint" race kit was a fun moment, and this year's Boston Marathon men's champion ran in ASICS — taken together, it makes me curious what they'll cook up next.
