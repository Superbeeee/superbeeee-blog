Today's main character is the first trainer in On's Cloudboom family, released late last year — **On Cloudboom Zone** (Zone, hereafter).

<!-- more -->

## 1. Introduction

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/03/on-cloudboom-zone-review/01-zone-cover.jpg" alt="">
<figcaption>📸 ：@ben_br_photography</figcaption>
</figure>

The Zone prototype was once rumored to be the Echo 4. The launch shut that down — it's a trainer. Lineage from Cloudboom, price not shy.

US MSRP: $210. No official Taiwan distribution, but the Taiwan site lists it at **NT$7,280**.

I bought from the Japanese official site at ¥25,300, which converts to roughly NT$5,465.

Since On hadn't previously released a Cloudboom-line trainer at the speed end, my pre-test guess was: fast intervals or long runs.

### Laces

<img src="/post/2025/03/on-cloudboom-zone-review/07-laces.jpg" alt="">

### Toe area

<img src="/post/2025/03/on-cloudboom-zone-review/08-toe-front.jpg" alt="">

The upper is closer to the Echo 3: a microfiber material with limited stretch but good breathability — and not as see-through as the Strike.

The midsole is Helion HF — a Pebax-based foam. The flagship Strike and Echo 3 both use Helion HF, just in different ratios. Strike skews more bouncy and explosive; the Zone feels more like the Echo 3 — a touch firmer.

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/03/on-cloudboom-zone-review/09-material-ref.png" alt="">
<figcaption>(Asked GPT to put together a quick reference of the materials.)</figcaption>
</figure>

Compared with other On models, the Zone has a noticeably reduced CloudTec® hole pattern in the outsole — only the heel keeps the cloud cutouts. To get aggressive stability, you sacrifice some of that signature design. Like the Echo 3, the Zone places a Speedboard (On's carbon plate) in the midfoot, but unlike the Echo 3 / Strike, it does not extend to the forefoot. Pure stability rather than propulsion — the push-off is on you. It's a trainer, after all.

### First impressions

The tongue is bonded to the upper, with raised foam to cushion the lacing. On foot it feels firm, less bouncy than the Strike. The heel sits stable — no slop. Strong first impression. I'll need to verify whether the heel rubs the Achilles like the Strike, and whether the laces stay put.

### Sizing & fit

Sizing was a gamble — Taiwan has no try-on locations — so I went with my usual size 10. On's last has been pretty consistent in my experience; very rarely have I needed to size up or down. The only exception was the Cloud 5, which needed a half-size up. Thankfully, US 10 fits.

## 4. Run Testing

### a. Road flat

**Location:** Dadaocheng riverside
**Distance:** 21K
**Pace:** 5:00/km

In this pace zone, the Speedboard adds tangible stability — every step lands consistently and rolls cleanly. It feels like a slightly softer Vaporfly 2 — enough rebound that you can put more into your stride during training. Post-run I didn't experience the loose-laces or Achilles-rubbing issues some Strike runners report on long sessions.

### b. Track intervals

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/03/on-cloudboom-zone-review/15-track-test.jpg" alt="">
<figcaption>📸 ：@kevinchung0829</figcaption>
</figure>

**Location:** Banti track
**Distance:** 1km × 4
**Pace:** 4:00/km (1km × 4, r 180s, 1:36/400m ± 2)

Coming off the long run I had the feel down. At higher pace the propulsion comes alive — and surprisingly, as I went faster and put more weight on the forefoot, the Zone returned more energy than expected. But because the carbon plate sits mainly in the midfoot, at higher pace the shoe felt less stable underfoot. For me, if I want consistent fast output, the Zone's sweet spot is slower than 4:00/km pace.

## 5. Honest Pros & Cons

### Pros

The half-Speedboard plus moderate cushioning gives speed-leaning runners stable, repeatable feedback while keeping you working — the carbon doesn't carry the entire run for you. The colorway is genuinely good-looking, and the design (99% training!) keeps reminding you to put in the work. The thick rubber outsole is durable and grips well even in wet conditions.

<figure>
<img src="/post/2025/03/on-cloudboom-zone-review/17-pros.jpg" alt="">
<figcaption>📸 ：@kevinchung0829</figcaption>
</figure>

### Cons

Cool laces, but I'd love something Nike-grade in lace-grip. And the price remains the Zone's hardest sell — bump the US MSRP $10 more and you're at the Puma Elite 3. There's a great quote I saw from an overseas reviewer: the Zone is a very bougie boston12. Compared with peers like the Nike Zoom Fly 6, ASICS Superblast 2, or even On's own Cloudmonster Hyper, the Zone is on the steeper side of the value curve.

## 6. Who Is This Shoe For?

Cloudboom-family fans, primarily. Functionally there's some overlap with the Cloudmonster Hyper, but the Hyper has no plate while the Zone has a Speedboard for stability — making the Zone better for runners who want a more grounded ride.

Good for fast intervals or long-run training, with steady steps and gradual progression. Half-marathon racing? Sure. For a full marathon I'd just go straight to the Strike. If your full is at 4:00+, the Cloudboom MAX would be the family pick.

## 7. Would I Recommend It?

Yes — provided your wallet can take the hit. The Zone is genuinely good-looking, and at the JPY exchange rate it's tolerable; at full US MSRP you're paying for the faith. On's lineup also doubles as everyday wear, which softens the financial sting a bit.
